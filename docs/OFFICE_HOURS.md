# Office Hours diagnostic

**Mode:** pre-product startup diagnostic. Because this is an unattended build, the six forcing questions are answered only from available evidence; unknowns remain explicit.

## 1. Demand reality

There is no direct demand evidence: no interviews, payments, renewal behavior, or observed workflows. The source establishes a plausible category and common renovation approvals, not product demand. A waitlist would not change this assessment. The next credible signal is payment for a live packet-preparation pilot.

## 2. Painful status quo

A homeowner reads a long declaration packet, emails a manager, locates an architectural form, assembles drawings/photos/material samples, gathers neighbor acknowledgment, and waits for a board or committee to flag omissions. Managers repeatedly answer the same questions and manually cite rules. The cost hypothesis is avoidable back-and-forth and longer approval cycle time; hours and rejection rates are unknown.

## 3. Desperate specificity

**Economic buyer:** the community manager responsible for 10–30 associations who is judged on response time, resident satisfaction, and board workload. An incomplete application creates resident escalation and another review cycle. **Primary user:** a homeowner with a contractor start date who cannot begin until the architectural review committee approves the project.

## 4. Narrowest paid wedge

A **$750 setup + $149/month architectural-request preflight** for one HOA and its three highest-volume project types. It answers from cited, versioned rules and produces a complete-packet checklist. No generic chatbot, city permitting, automated board decision, or submission portal in the wedge.

## 5. Observation and validation

No unassisted use has been observed. Recruit five portfolio managers and watch each process one anonymized recently submitted fence request without coaching. Measure source documents opened, clarification messages, completion time, missed requirements, citation corrections, and whether the final packet is accepted for committee review. Then offer a paid 30-day concierge pilot to three managers.

## 6. Future-fit

If AI-generated answers become abundant, trustworthy provenance becomes more valuable. ArcReady wins only if every answer is tied to the currently effective rule version and a human can correct the rule library. A generic conversational layer becomes less defensible; the rule-ingestion, review history, and manager workflow become more essential.

## Position and assignment

The specific paperwork preflight is worth testing; “ChatGPT for every HOA question” is not. The immediate assignment is to obtain three redacted rejected applications and observe five managers processing one request each before adding AI or integrations.
